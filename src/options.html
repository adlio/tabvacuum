Stale Tabs

Tabs are closed, not archived. Unsaved changes may be lost.

Automatic cleanup checks once an hour, in normal windows only. Turning it on closes nothing right away: the first check is at the next hour. It always keeps each window's active tab (no matter how long since it was active), pinned tabs, tabs playing audio, the last tab in a window, and tabs whose age is unknown.

A tab's age is measured from when you last used it. On Firefox that is the last time you viewed the tab; on Chrome it is the last time you selected (activated) it, so the clock starts when you switch to a tab, not when you leave it. A tab you switch away from can qualify once more than the threshold has passed.

After a browser restart, cleanup waits for the next hourly check and does not catch up. After your device sleeps, at most one check runs when it wakes; missed hours are not replayed. To recover a closed tab, use your browser's Reopen Closed Tab.

Duplicate Detection

When a new tab opens a page that is already open in this kind of window (and the same container), switch to the open tab and close the new one. Only new tabs are checked. A tab opened from its match (Duplicate Tab, or a link to the same page) is kept, and so is a page you reopen within a minute of it being closed, so Reopen Closed Tab (Ctrl+Shift+T, or Cmd+Shift+T on a Mac) undoes a switch.

Ask first shows "Already open" in the new tab, with Switch and Keep both. Keep both loads the page and leaves the tab alone.

A line matches a page when the page's address starts with it, or when the line is the page's host name (such as docs.example.com). Write /pattern/ for a regular expression.
